[0001] [TITLE] Genetic association of lipids and lipid-lowering drug target genes with breast cancer

[0002] [ABSTRACT] Background

[0003] [ABSTRACT] Although several preclinical and epidemiological studies have shown that blood lipids and lipid-lowering drugs can reduce the risk of breast cancer, this finding remains controversial. This study aimed to explore the causal relationship between dyslipidemia,lipid-lowering drugs, and breast cancer. We also aimed to evaluate the potential impact of lipid-lowering drug targets on breast cancer.

[0004] [ABSTRACT] Method

[0005] [ABSTRACT] Data of 431 lipid- and lipid-related phenotypes were obtained from genome-wide association study (GWAS), and mendelian randomization (MR) analyses were performed using two independent breast cancer datasets as endpoints. Genetic variants associated with genes encoding lipid-lowering drug targets were extracted from the Global Lipid Genetics Consortium. Expression quantitative trait loci data in relevant tissues were used to further validate lipid-lowering drug targets that reached significance and combined with bioinformatics approaches for molecular expression and prognostic exploration. Further mediation analyses were performed to explore potential mediators.

[0006] [ABSTRACT] Result

[0007] [ABSTRACT] In two independent datasets, phosphatidylcholine (18:1_0:0 levels) was associated with breast cancer risk (discovery: odds ratio (OR) = 1.255 [95% confidence interval (CI) 1.120–1.406]; p = 8.936 × 10–5, replication: OR = 1.016 [95% CI, 1.003–1.030]; p = 0.017), HMG- CoA reductase (HMGCR) inhibition was genetically modeled and associated with a reduced risk of breast cancer (discovery: OR = 0.833 [95% CI 0.752–0.923], p = 5.12 × 10–4; replication: OR = 0.975 [95% CI 0.960–0.990], p = 1.65 × 10–3). There was a significant MR correlation between HMGCR expression in whole blood and breast cancer (OR = 1.11 [95% 1.01–1.22] p = 0.04). Bioinformatics analysis revealed that HMGCR expression higher in breast cancer tissues than in normal tissues, along with poor overall survival and relapse-free survival, and was associated with multiple immune cell infiltration. Finally, the mediation analysis showed that HMGCR inhibitors affected breast cancer through different immune cell phenotypes and C-reactive protein levels.

[0008] [ABSTRACT] Conclusion

[0009] [ABSTRACT] In this study, we found for the first time that phosphatidylcholine (18:1_0:0) levels are associated with breast cancer risk. We found that HMGCR inhibitors are associated with a reduced risk of breast cancer, and part of their action may be through pathways other than lipid-lowering, including modulation of immune function and reduction of inflammation represented by C-reactive protein levels.

[0012] [INTRO] Introduction

[0013] [INTRO] Breast cancer (BC) is one of the most common malignancies worldwide, and the most common malignancy in women. In 2020, over 2.3 million women were diagnosed with BC globally, with 685,000 deaths. Circulating lipids play an important role in endocrine tumors by enhancing cell proliferation and migration. However, owing to the influence of confounding factors in clinical studies, current evidence of the relationship between circulating lipids and BC risk is conflicting. Lipid-lowering drugs are commonly used to prevent cardiovascular diseases and may improve cardiovascular outcomes. Drug pleiotropy has led to the use of lipid-lowering drugs for a variety of diseases, and their relationship with oncological diseases has been extensively studied. Preclinical data have shown that lipid-lowering drugs attenuate the inflammatory response, inhibit the proliferation of cancer cells, and inhibit the progression and micrometastasis of BC. Lipid-lowering drugs also reduce BC recurrence and have a protective effect on prognosis. However, there is limited evidence from previous observational clinical studies showing that lipid-lowering drugs are associated with the risk of BC development.

[0014] [INTRO] Lipid-lowering drugs are widely used to prevent hyperlipidemia, atherosclerosis, and cardiovascular diseases. During BC treatment, patients are prone to dyslipidemia, especially during endocrine therapy. For example, tamoxifen, letrozole, and other drugs may affect the blood lipid levels of patients with BC to varying degrees and increase the risk of cardiovascular diseases and metabolic syndrome. Therefore, the relationship between lipid-lowering drugs and BC needs to be clarified. Clarifying the relationship between lipid-lowering drugs and BC is not only of scientific research significance but also of practical significance for the prevention and prognosis of BC. In a multicenter cohort study involving 7528 white women in the United States, participants who used lipid-lowering drugs had a significantly lower risk of BC than those who did not.These findings reveal the potential application of lipid-lowering drugs in the prevention of BC. However, long-term use of lipid-lowering drugs was not associated with BC risk in an analysis of the Cancer Prevention Study II Nutrition Cohort based on a 10-year follow-up period. This inconsistency in the results of these studies may be because of the complex polygenic and multifactorial nature of BC, which cannot be completely overcome by observational and traditional analyses.

[0015] [INTRO] There are limitations to clinical studies that lead to biased results, such as confounding factor interference and reverse causality interference, ethical issues in the design of experiments with limited observation times, and potential adverse effects that may result from prolonged administration of the drug to the subjects. Owing to the increasing popularity of genome-wide association studies (GWAS), mendelian randomization (MR), an epidemiological method that avoids confounding and reverse causation through the use of genetic variation in a population, may be an effective way to overcome the above problems.This is similar to a randomized controlled trial in which participants are randomly assigned to either the experimental or control group. Simultaneously, MR is not subject to reverse causation because the genetic variation remains constant throughout life. It can also predict drug effects based on mutations in risk factors or drug target genes as tools. This provides preliminary evidence of causality to inform subsequent intervention studies, drug monitoring, and public health approaches. MR studies have been widely used in rheumatic, neurological, and oncological diseases.

[0016] [INTRO] The widespread use of lipid-lowering drugs in the treatment of cardiovascular diseases has raised concerns regarding their potential association with the risk of BC. The urgency and importance of studying the relationship between circulating lipids, lipid-lowering drugs, and BC risk are self-evident.In this study, health management for the patient, doctor decision-making, and public health policy are of great significance, and we analyzed the influence of the characteristics of BC and discussed the lipid-lowering drug targets for BC subtypes and their potential impact.

[0017] [METHODS] Materials and methods

[0018] [METHODS] Study design

[0019] [METHODS] We first assessed the causal association of 431 lipid and lipid-related phenotypes with BC using two-sample MR analysis. We then performed MR analyses of 9 commonly used lipid-lowering drug targets with BC and summary-data-based Mendelian randomization (SMR) analyses of important lipid-lowering drug targets to validate the results, followed by the application of multiple bioinformatics methods to analyze lipid-lowering drug targets and mediation analyses to explore potential mediators.

[0021] [METHODS] There are three basic assumptions behind the MR approach: (1) genetic variation and exposure are closely related ("correlation"); (2) genetic variation is independent of confounders affecting the relationship between exposure and outcome ("independence"); and (3) genetic variation only influences outcome through exposure of interest of exposure ("exclusion restriction") affecting outcome. This study followed the enhanced reporting of observational studies in the Mendelian epidemiology guidelines for randomized reporting (Table S1). Figure 1 presents an outline of the study flow. Data sources were based on publicly available summary-level data from the GWAS study and expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) study, which were desensitized before uploading and did not involve personal privacy or identifiable information. This study did not involve confidential information and data and informed consent authorization of the institution; thus, no approval from the ethical review board was required. The details of these datasets are presented in Table S2.

[0022] [METHODS] GWAS data sources of exposure

[0023] [METHODS] A total of 431 lipid and lipid-related phenotypes were used as exposures (Table S3-S7), of which 179 lipids were obtained from a study published in 2023 that provided pooled GWAS data for each metabolite, with sample sizes ranging from 5172 to 7174. Furthermore, 249 lipids and lipid-related profiles were obtained from a study published in 2022. In this study, sample sizes ranged from 110,051 to 115,082 for the different metabolites. Finally, GWAS summary statistics for low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C), total cholesterol (TC), and triglycerides (TG) were obtained from a GWAS meta-analysis conducted by the Global Lipids Genetics Consortium, a program with sample sizes ranging from 173,082 to 187,365.

[0024] [METHODS] GWAS data sources for outcomes

[0025] [METHODS] Summary statistics for outcomes were obtained from two separate GWAS datasets from the Breast Cancer Association Consortium (BCAC) and UK Biobank (UKB). The primary outcome was BC, and the secondary outcomes were the subtypes of BC, including (estrogen receptor (ER)-positive and ER-negative). The BCAC is an international collaborative project initiated in 2005 to study genetic susceptibility to BC and includes 122,977 female cases and 105,974 female controls of European origin. In the BCAC, BC cases were mainly from hospitals and cancer registries. To replicate the primary outcome analyses, we obtained another BC GWAS dataset from the UK Biobank, which included 13,879 cases and 198,523 controls, all of whom were of the European origin38. Previous studies have described the study designs, participants, and quality control (QC) methods in detail.

[0026] [METHODS] Data filtration and genetic instrument selection

[0027] [METHODS] In our analysis of lipids and BC risk, we set an inclusion threshold for single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) (p < 5 × 10–8), in addition to an r2 threshold of 0.001 to prevent linkage disequilibrium (LD) and a distance of 10,000 Kb for SNPs. The strength of each genetic variant was assessed using F-statistical analysis (calculated as F = β^2 / SE^2, where β is the allelic effect value and SE is the standard error) to test the hypothesis of correlation. Typically, an F-statistic > 10 indicates the absence of weak instrumental bias.

[0030] [METHODS] Common lipid-lowering drugs and novel therapies include statins, ezetimibe, PCSK9 inhibitors, bile acid sequestrants, mipomersen, fibrates, angiopoietin-like 3 (ANGPTL3) inhibitors, and antisense oligonucleotides targeting apolipoprotein C-III (APOC3) mRNA (Table 1). The DrugBank database (https://go.drugbank. com/) and related studies were used to identify the genes encoding the pharmacological targets of these drugs. Based on their primary pharmacological effects, these target genes were further categorized into low-density lipoprotein (LDL)-C lowering target genes (LDL receptor (LDLR), HMG-CoA reductase (HMGCR), Niemann-Pick C1-like protein 1 (NPC1L1), PCSK9, apolipoprotein B-100 (APOB), ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters G5 (ABCG5) and G8 (ABCG8)), and TG-lowering target genes (lipoprotein lipase (LPL), ANGPTL3, and APOC3) (Table 1).

[0031] [METHODS] For the selection methodology of genetic variation tools, we referred to previous similar studies. In a GWAS meta-analysis by the Global Lipids Genetics Consortium, SNPs identified within the corresponding genes (± 100 kb of the gene location) and strongly associated with LDL-C or TG levels in a genome-wide sense (p < 5 × 10–8), clustered further to an LD threshold of r2 < 0.30 and a physical distance threshold of 100 kb, were selected as proxies for lipid-lowering drug targets. Owing to the proximity of the ABCG5 and ABCG8 genes (ABCG5: chr2:44,039,611-44,065,978 [ensembl]; ABCG8: chr2:44,066,110-44,105,605 [ensembl]), variants near these genes were combined in our analysis. Nine drug targets were identified: i.e. HMGCR, NPC1L1, PCSK9, APOB, ABCG5/ABCG8, LDLR, ANGPTL3, APOC3, and LPL (Table S8).

[0032] [METHODS] For drug targets important for BC risk in the MR analysis, we used eQTLs available for drug target genes as proxies for exposure to each lipid-lowering drug target. eQTLs were obtained at the summary level from the eQTLGen consortium (https://www.eqtlgen.org/) or the GTEx consortium V8 (https://gtexportal.org/), the details of which are provided in Table S2. We identified common (minor allele frequency [MAF] < 1%) SNPs of eQTLs significantly associated with HMGCR expression in blood as well as NPC1L1 expression in adipose tissue (p < 5.0 × 10–8), and only cis-eQTLs were included in the present study to generate the genetic instrumentation, which were defined as encoding eQTL within 1 Mb on either side of the gene.

[0033] [METHODS] Survival analysis

[0034] [METHODS] The Kaplan–Meier plotter database (http://kmplot.com) is a survival analysis tool that integrates data from public databases, including GEO (Gene Expression Omnibus), EGA (European Genome-phenome Archive), TCGA (The Cancer Genome Atlas), and other public databases for breast cancer (BC) patients. This tool can be used to assess disease progression. The 'mRNA gene chip' module was employed to investigate the prognostic value of HMGCR in BC.

[0035] [METHODS] TME and immune cell infiltration

[0036] [METHODS] We calculated the tumor microenvironment (TME) scores for the BC samples using the ‘estimate’ package. We then compared these differences based on HMGCR expression levels. Using the single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm available in the R package—GSVA, we evaluated 24 immune cells in the BC tissue samples and assessed the correlation between HMGCR expression and them using the spearman analysis using the ‘ggplot2’ package. We used the ‘ggplot2’ and ‘pheatmap’ packages to visualize the correlations.

[0037] [METHODS] Statistical analysis

[0038] [METHODS] In analyses where only a single SNP was available for the construction of instrumental variables, the Wald ratio method was used to derive MR estimates. If multiple SNPs were available to construct instrumental variables, the inverse variance weighting (IVW) method was used as the primary analysis method, because it provided the most accurate and robust estimates.

[0039] [METHODS] To validate the selection of genetic variants as drug targets, positive control analyses were performed using coronary artery disease (CAD) as the endpoint.

[0040] [METHODS] We also performed a series of sensitivity analyses owing to their resilience to violations of certain assumptions underlying the MR study. Heterogeneity and multivalence among the SNPs were assessed using Cochran's Q test and the MR-Egger intercept test. For MR sensitivity analyses, multiple validity residuals and outliers (MR-PRESSO) were used to reduce the bias caused by correlation level pleiotropy. A significance level of p < 0.005 (0.05/9) using Bonferroni correction was used to adjust for multiple testing of the nine drug targets. For the other analyses, the observed 2-sided p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses described above were performed using 'TwoSampleMR, 'MRPRESSO' in R (version 4.3.0).

[0041] [METHODS] As lipid-lowering drugs have anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties, and these two properties are closely related to the development of BC, we used a "two-step MR" approach to conduct mediation analysis to explore whether there is a mediating effect (exposure-mediator-outcome pathway) between lipid-lowering drug targets and BC. We used the "coefficient product" method to evaluate the indirect effect of each potential mediator, the effect of lipid-lowering drug targets on BC risk, and the proportion of mediators.

[0042] [RESULTS] Results

[0043] [RESULTS] Lipid traits and BC risk

[0044] [RESULTS] In the discovery dataset, we identified two lipid traits that were causally associated with BC (p < 1.31 × 10–4) and an increase in the level of the phosphatidylcholine (18:1_0:0) was associated with BC risk (odds ratio (OR) = 1.255 [95% confidence interval (CI) 1.120–1.406]; p = 8.936E−05), a finding that was similarly confirmed by the replication cohort (OR = 1.016 [95% CI, 1.003–1.030]; p = 0.017). An increase in associated HDL_C levels was associated with an elevated BC risk (OR = 1.170 [95% CI 1.082–1.264]; p = 7.877E−05). In the replication dataset, this finding was not significant (OR = 1.000 [95% CI, 0.991–1.008]; p = 0.949). The comprehensive MR analysis results for lipid traits are presented in the supplementary tables (S9-S10), while the results of the heterogeneity and pleiotropy tests are detailed in the supplementary tables (S11-S12).

[0047] [RESULTS] In the discovery dataset, HMGCR target inhibition was significantly associated with a lower risk of BC (OR = 0.83 [95% CI 0.752–0.923], p = 5.12 × 10–4) (Fig. 2A). In the replication dataset, we validated this finding (OR = 0.97 [95% CI 0.960–0.990] p = 1.65 × 10–3) (Fig. 2B). In the discovery dataset, NPC1L1 target inhibition was significantly associated with a reduced risk of BC (OR = 0.72 [95% CI 0.575–0.903], p = 4.55 × 10–3), but analyses obtained from the dis replication dataset showed the opposite trend (OR = 1.05 [95% CI 1.024–1.078], p = 1.63 × 10–3) (Fig. 2A, B). PCSK9 target inhibition was significantly associated with a reduced risk of BC in the discovery dataset (OR = 0.90 [95% CI 0.842–0.969], p = 4.55 × 10–3), but this observation was not validated in the replication dataset (OR = 0.99 [95% CI 0.986–1.002], p = 0.20) (Fig. 2A, B). Results for the other five lipid-lowering drug targets (APOB, ABCG5/ABCG8, LPL, ANGPTL3, and APOC3) failed to be corrected by p-value (Fig. 2A, B). Cochran's Q test with the MR-Egger intercept test did not reveal evidence of heterogeneity or multiplicity, which strengthened the causal inference of exposure and outcomes (Tables S13 and S14). Further analyses showed that HMGCR target inhibition was primarily associated with a reduced risk of ER-positive BC (OR: 0.84 [95% CI 0.743–0.949], p = 0.005), and the results of the analyses in the ER-negative BC dataset were not significant (p = 0.07) (Table S15). The positive control studies have demonstrated a clear association between the exposure levels of each drug and coronary heart disease, further validating the effectiveness of the selected genetic instruments (Table S16).

[0048] [RESULTS] Gene expression and BC risk

[0049] [RESULTS] The results of the target analyses were validated using the SMR approach. Results revealed that a 1-SD increase in blood tissue HMGCR expression was associated with a higher risk of BC (OR = 1.11 [CI 95% 1.01–1.22] p = 0.04). Furthermore, given the inconsistent findings on the relationship between enhanced genetic variation in NPC1L1 and BC risk, we further explored this relationship using NPC1L1 expression in adipose tissue; however, the analysis was not significant (OR = 1.04 [CI 95% 1.00–1.08] p = 0.07) (Table S17). The analysis of PCSK9 in blood tissue and breast cancer did not yield statistically significant results (OR = 1.00 [CI 95% 0.93–1.08] p = 0.92).

[0052] [RESULTS] First, we obtained the expression profiles of HMGCR in 33 human tumors from the TIMER2.0 database (http://timer.comp-genomics.org/timer/) and found that HMGCR was aberrantly expressed in many types of human tumors, including BC, indicating that HMGCR is a tumor-associated gene (Fig. 3A). We compared the expression profile of HMGCR in BC tissues with that in normal tissues and found that HMGCR expression was significantly increased in BC. The immunohistochemistry data presented in Fig. 3E are derived from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA). The results demonstrate an increase in HMGCR expression compared to the control group. We then performed survival analyses based on survival time and HMGCR expression profiles in the Kaplan–Meier Plotter database (http://kmplot.com/analysis/), which showed that a high HMGCR expression group was associated with a poorer prognosis for patients (Fig. 3C, D). Overall, HMGCR expression was elevated in BC tissues, indicating a poor prognosis.

[0056] [RESULTS] Mediation analysis

[0057] [RESULTS] Considering that differential expression of HMGCR targets was significantly associated with multiple immune cell infiltrations, it is reasonable to speculate that inhibitors of HMGCR targets may lead to a reduced risk of BC by altering immune function. Further analyses were performed to explore potential mediators between inhibitors of HMGCR targets and BC causality. We performed mediation analyses of 731 immune cell phenotypes using GWAS data as mediators. The GWAS Catalog (GCST90001391–GCST90002121) summarizes the GWAS statistics for each immune characteristic. It contains comprehensive data collected from 3757 Europeans and consists of 731 immune phenotypes.

[0059] [RESULTS] Our analysis showed that HMGCR-targeting inhibitors were positively associated with 61 immune cell phenotypes (OR > 1, p < 0.05) and the remaining significant 52 traits were negatively associated with HMGCR-targeting inhibitors (OR < 1, p < 0.05) (Table S18). Subsequently, in our MR analysis of immune cell phenotypes and BC, we found that 18 phenotypes were positively associated with the risk of BC (OR > 1, p < 0.05), and 22 immune cell phenotypes were associated with a reduced risk of BC (OR < 1, p < 0.05) (Fig. 5A, B) (Table S19). In subsequent calculations, the HMGCR target inhibitors were shown to be effective in modulating three immune cell phenotypes (including " IgD + CD38 + B cell Absolute Count " (mediation proportion: 7.72%), "Transitional B cell Absolute Count" (mediation proportion: 12.3%), "HLA DR on CD33- HLA DR + " (mediation proportion: 9.99%)) to reduce the risk of BC.

[0060] [RESULTS] GWAS data for 91 circulating inflammatory proteins were summarized from a meta-analysis including 14,824 participants of the European descent from 11 independent cohorts (accession numbers GCST90274758 to GCST90274848). Furthermore, we added C-reactive protein (CRP), a commonly used inflammatory marker, as it was not included in this study. The results showed that HMGCR inhibitors had a positive causal relationship with eight inflammatory factors (OR > 1, p < 0.05) and a negative causal relationship with 12 inflammatory factor phenotypes (OR < 1, p < 0.05) (Table S20). Further analysis suggested that five inflammatory factor phenotypes were positively causally associated with BC and eight inflammatory factor phenotypes were negatively causally associated with BC (Fig. 5C, D) (Table S21). We found that only CRP level (mediation ratio: 7.42%) had a significant mediating effect.

[0061] [DISCUSS] Discussion

[0062] [DISCUSS] In this study, we first performed a MR analysis of 431 lipid and lipid-related phenotypes in relation to BC and found that phosphatidylcholine (18:1_0:0) levels were positively correlated with the risk of BC. Subsequently, In the drug-target analysis, HMGCR was identified as a drug target that significantly reduces the risk of BC. This finding was validated using different methods for constructing genetic instruments and two independent BC datasets. Our study provides strong evidence that HMGCR is a promising drug target for reducing the risk of BC, particularly ER-positive BC.

[0063] [DISCUSS] By integrating multiple bioinformatics methods, we performed further exploratory analyses to identify the biological functions and potential regulatory mechanisms of HMGCR in BC.We found that the expression level of HMGCR was upregulated in BC, high expression of HMGCR was correlated with poorer overall survival(OS) and relapse-free survival(RFS) in BC, and the expression level of HMGCR was correlated with a variety of immune-infiltrating cells. Based on this finding, we conducted a mediation analysis and found that HMGCR inhibitors reduced the risk of BC by affecting a variety of immune cell phenotypes and regulating CRP levels.

[0064] [DISCUSS] The effects of elevated LDL-C, TG, and TC levels on the risk of total and ER-positive BCs were markedly heterogeneous according to the study design, menopausal status, and subject body mass index. Regarding the inherent bias of residual confounding and reverse causation in observational studies, MR is an increasingly used methodology that uses genetic variants as instrumental variables to draw definitive conclusions regarding the causality of the association between exposure and disease. MR takes advantage of the random assignment of genetic alleles affecting exposure, which can avoid reverse causality bias and minimize confounding by other determinants in a manner similar to that of randomized controlled trials. In this study, we minimized the bias caused by observational study confounders. The results of previous MR studies have been controversial and we adopted a more rigorous analytical strategy using two independent GWAS datasets for validation. Based on the available evidence, we found that LDL-C, TG, and TC may not be considered causative risk factors for BC, implying that LDL-C, TG, and TC levels do not independently affect BC, which is consistent with the results of previous MR and observational studies. Meanwhile, we found for the first time that phosphatidylcholine (18:1_0:0) levels were associated with BC risk using GWAS data from 428 liposomes.

[0065] [DISCUSS] Compared with a previous MR study reported by SUN, we used two independent sets of GWAS results data and further validated them using SMR analysis to extend the analysis to nine common lipid-lowering drug targets. The results of this study support the idea that HMGCR inhibitors can reduce the risk of BC, and HMGCR inhibitors may be recommended to reduce the incidence of BC, especially ER-positive BC, in women with hyperlipidemia. This is consistent with the results of a multicenter prospective cohort study and preclinical evidence that statins can reduce breast cancer cell growth and induce apoptosis in various experimental models. In addition, several epidemiological studies have confirmed the correlation between the use of statins and the reduction in the risk of disease recurrence after adjuvant therapy, and the use of cholesterol-lowering drugs at the same time of adjuvant endocrine therapy in patients with hormone receptor-positive early breast cancer may have a certain role in the prevention of breast cancer recurrence. In our study, lipid-lowering agents were associated with a reduced risk of BC in ER + , a finding that provides an initial qualification of the population for whom lipid-lowering agents would benefit. Consistent is previous studies reporting that statins may be particularly associated with ER + BC. Cholesterol is the building block of hormones (such as estrogen and progesterone) and the recently discovered selective endogenous estrogen receptor modulator (SERM), 27-hydroxycholesterol, which is associated with ER-dependent tumor proliferation and immune escape. Therefore, inhibition of cholesterol synthesis may lead to decreased cell proliferation in hormone receptor-positive breast tumors.

[0066] [DISCUSS] Statins are HMGCR inhibitors, a class of drugs containing a naphthyl ring backbone structure that regulate plasma lipoprotein concentrations by inhibiting cholesterol synthesis. Because the hydrophilic moiety of statins resembles the HMG portion of the substrate, statins can be used as competitive inhibitors of HMG-CoA. Statins play a role in diseases such as diabetes mellitus, neurological disorders, and neoplasms. Such drugs have the potential to alter the patterns of cholesterol synthesis and isoprenylation by controlling the mevalonate pathway, thereby affecting the functions of proteins and oncoproteins that regulate proliferation, migration, invasion, cell cycle, cell fate, and apoptosis. Furthermore, HMGCR inhibitors have been demonstrated to possess notable anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory properties, which ultimately result in anti-tumour effects. Specifically, such inhibitors are capable of engaging the immune system, restoring the capacity to monitor tumour cells and enhancing the immunogenicity of tumour cells, thereby enhancing the anti-tumour response of the immune system. Our preliminary studies indicate a potential correlation between HMGCR expression and the presence of immune cells in the breast cancer tumour microenvironment. This finding is of particular interest as the level of cholesterol synthesis and metabolism exerts a profound effect on the function of immune cells, including their ability to migrate to the tumour site, their recognition of cancer cells, and the signalling pathways within the immune cells. These changes may upset the balance between immunosuppression and anti-tumour immune responses, which in turn has important implications for breast cancer (BC) risk.

[0067] [DISCUSS] Moreover, previous studies have identified the cholesterol metabolite 27-hydroxycholesterol (27HC) as a principal mediator of cholesterol's influence on the growth and progression of breast tumours. 27HC contributes to tumour progression by acting as a ligand for the liver X receptor in myeloid immune cells, thereby establishing an immunosuppressive programme and modifying the tumour microenvironment through exosome-dependent and non-exosome-independent mechanisms. Choe et al. demonstrated that atorvastatin, an HMGCR inhibitor, effectively enhanced the anti-tumour immune response and inhibited the immune escape of T cells by decreasing PD-L1 expression and reducing the secretion of extracellular vesicles (EVs). The mediator MR analysis in this study suggests that HMGCR inhibitors may reduce the incidence of BC by modulating three immune phenotypes: IgD + CD38 + B cell absolute count, transitional B cell absolute count, and HLA DR in CD33- HLA DR + myeloid cells. We did not identify their mechanism of action in BC; however, in some cases, they may play a role in immune function modulation.

[0068] [DISCUSS] Chronic inflammation promotes cancer development. Mechanisms by which inflammation promotes tumorigenesis include the induction of growth factors that produce proliferation, pro-angiogenic growth factors, and the promotion of the epithelial-mesenchymal transition. Specifically, chronic inflammation may increase the risk of BC through activation of redox-sensitive transcription factors, augmentation of local estrogen production, and induction of angiogenesis. Statins exert anti-inflammatory effects. The main mechanisms include inhibition of neutrophil migration, oxidative stress, suppression of the intrinsic immune response, and increased expression of pro-inflammatory cytokines (interleukin (IL)-10, hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), and transforming growth factor β1 (TGF-β1). Our study confirmed that HMGCR can affect multiple inflammatory factor phenotypes and that multiple inflammatory phenotypes are associated with the risk of BC, with CRP as a significant mediator.

[0069] [DISCUSS] Our study also has some limitations. MR estimates represent long-term modulation of disease risk by drug targets, which may suggest that they are not capable of reflecting the relationship between lipid-lowering drugs and BC risk over time; therefore, the results observed in clinical studies may differ from the results of the present study. Horizontal pleiotropy could not be completely ruled out in the present study despite the fact that various sensitivity analyses were performed to test the hypotheses of the MR analysis. Finally, because all data in our study were obtained from individuals of the European ancestry, this somewhat circumvents the heterogeneity that exists between different ethnic groups; however, the results of this study may not be applicable to other ethnic groups.

[0070] [DISCUSS] In conclusion, this study is the first to demonstrate that phosphatidylcholine (18:1_0:0) levels are associated with the risk of BC, suggesting that HMGCR inhibitors are associated with a reduced risk of BC, and part of the mechanism may be through pathways other than lipid-lowering efficacy, such as modulating immune function and inflammatory factors. This study provides evidence for further exploration of the relationship between HMGCR inhibitors and BC.
